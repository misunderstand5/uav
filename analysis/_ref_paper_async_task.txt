Asynchronous Task Scheduling and Resource
Allocation for UA V -Enabled Mobile Edge
Computing Networks
Xiuling Zhang , Riheng Jia , Member , IEEE, Quanjun Yin , Zhonglong Zheng , Member , IEEE,
and Minglu Li , Fellow, IEEE
Abstract—Mobile edge computing (MEC) is promising in han-
dling delay-sensitive or resource-intensive tasks in mobile internet.
Existing system schedulers in MEC networks usually schedule all
service providers in a synchronous manner, which may not suit
the practical scenario where tasks are randomly generated and
require different computational resources and service times. In
this work, we jointly optimize the task scheduling and resource
allocation in an unmanned aerial vehicle (UA V)-enabled MEC
network, where multiple UA Vs asynchronously and cooperatively
deliver task ofﬂoading and computing services to edge devices
(EDs), for maximizing the average per-UA V energy utility and
minimizing the overall task missing ratio. To enhance schedul-
ing efﬁciency and jointly optimize task scheduling and resource
allocation, we develop an asynchronous layered multi-agent proxi-
mal policy optimization (AL-MAPPO) algorithm, by incorporating
the multi-UA V asynchronous action execution mechanism and a
discrete-continuous layered action space into the general MAPPO
framework. AL-MAPPO enables each UA V to perform ﬂexible task
scheduling and ﬁne-grained resource allocation asynchronously.
Extensive trace-driven simulations based on Alibaba Cluster Data
V2017 validate the effectiveness of AL-MAPPO, compared with
several baseline algorithms.
Index Terms —Mobile edge computing, asynchronous system
operation, multi-agent reinforcement learning, task scheduling,
resource allocation.

a task as a missed task. For each task, minimizing the time

proximity, deadline slack, and future capacity. We summarize
the main challenges of problem-solving as follows.
The exact ED-side information is inaccessible to UA Vs: In
this work, the exact ED-side information, e.g., when will an
ED generate a certain request and what type of computing
resource will the corresponding task need, is inaccessible to
UA Vs, since requests are usually randomly generated across
the EDs in practical scenarios, which show unpredictability and
dynamics. It is difﬁcult for UA Vs to make appropriate decisions
of task scheduling and resource allocation without knowing the
exact ED-side information.
The trade-off between energy utility and task missing ratio:
To reduce the number of missed tasks, UA Vs need to prioritize
the tasks with early deadlines. However, it may take a long time
for the UA V to move to a far-away ED to collect the task with an
early deadline, which decreases the UA V’s energy utility since
much energy is spent on movement rather than computation.
With limited onboard energy for both the movement and com-
putation, the lower energy utility usually indicates the fewer
tasks completed within a ﬁxed horizon.
Multiple UA Vs operate asynchronously and cooperatively
within the network: Both task collection and computation times
vary. The collection time depends on the UA V –ED distance and
ofﬂoading delay, while the computation time depends on the
allocated computational resources. This yields asynchronous
operation of available UA Vs per slot, requiring effective per-
UA V task scheduling and resource allocation policies to balance
task demand and resource supply. In addition, since multiple

To handle those challenges, we introduce the multi-agent

decisions to maximize the total rewards by only accessing their
local observations. According to the developed Dec-POMDP ,
we propose an asynchronous layered multi-agent proximal pol-

MAPPO framework [19], leveraging the centralized training
and decentralized execution (CTDE) framework. We summarize
main contributions as follows.r This paper makes an effort to design an asynchronous

optimize online task scheduling and resource allocation in
a decentralized and dynamic UA V –MEC system without

formulated as a Dec-POMDP , and a CTDE-based MARL

scheduling of multiple UA Vs in this environment.r We develop the AL-MAPPO algorithm by designing a

its task scheduling and resource allocation by interact-

maximize each UA V’s energy utility on average and mini-

pared with several baseline algorithms. Additionally, load
balancing among UA Vs is evaluated to further demonstrate
the performance of our algorithm.

III. S YSTEM MODEL
We consider a UA V -enabled MECN that consists of M ter-
restrial EDs and N rotary-wing UA Vs carrying MEC servers,
where EDs may ofﬂoad their tasks to UA Vs for processing. Key
notations are summarized in Table II. In general, the system
operates as follows: 1) There is a BS located in the network
which continuously collects requests from EDs, for example,
via multi-hop transmission. Speciﬁcally, in each time slot, the
EDs that have generated tasks upload their task requests to
the BS over a Time Division Multiple Access (TDMA)-based
uplink [36], [37]. After collecting all requests within the current
slot, the BS aggregates them into a single control-plane packet
and broadcasts this packet to all UA Vs over a common downlink
control channel; 2) Upon receiving and decoding the aggregated
packet, each UA V updates its local view of outstanding requests
and, according to its own strategy, decides whether to serve a
request and which one to select, based on the request information
and its available computing resources and energy; 3) Once the
UA V determines to serve a certain request, it moves close to
the ED that sends the request, collects the corresponding task,
and allocates the appropriate computing resource; 4) Each UA V
repeats Steps 2-3 until all UA Vs drain out their batteries or the
pre-deﬁned maximum system operation time is reached.

since the time durations for executing various actions (e.g., the

D. Design and Implementation of AL-MAPPO
Architecture: The proposed AL-MAPPO algorithm is devel-
oped based on the MAPPO framework by incorporating two
policy networks and one critic network. It utilizes a centralized
critic to process the global states of UA Vs to guide policy
training for each UA V . During the decentralized action execution
phase, each UA V selects the action according to its own local
observation and executes the chosen action asynchronously. The
algorithm framework is shown in Fig. 3, where three neural
networks are deployed with the same network architecture.
Speciﬁcally, the share critic network is exploited to evaluate
the value of each global state, and the share policy networks
consisting of discrete and continuous policy networks are used
to output the action tuple of each UA V . All UA Vs share the
same policy networks πθd(·|o) and πθc(·|o,a). Also, all UA Vs
share the same critic network Vϕ(s). Note that there is only one

training episode is terminated. Note that, each training episode

VII. C ONCLUSION
In this work, we maximized the energy utility of each UA V
on average while minimizing the overall task missing ratio in
a general multi-UA V enabled MECN, by jointly optimizing
the task scheduling and resource allocation of each UA V . To
address the inaccessibility to the exact ED-side information
and the asynchronous operation of each UA V , we designed
a MARL based algorithm called AL-MAPPO, which enables
UA Vs to cooperatively learn their optimal strategies of request
selection and resource allocation, by asynchronously interacting
with the dynamic MEC environment. Extensive experiments
based on real-world data trace demonstrate that our algorithm
outperforms several baselines in terms of task missing ratio and
average task completion time. We also evaluated load balancing
among UA Vs in energy, resource utility, and throughput under
AL-MAPPO, further highlighting its superiority. In the future,
we will explore service-heterogeneous UA V -enabled MECNs,
where heterogeneous UA Vs will support various service types by
using intelligent trajectory planning strategies to meet broader
service demands.
